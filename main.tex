\begin{abstract}
Spatial transcriptomics (ST) deconvolution tools (e.g., \texttt{cell2location}, \texttt{RCTD}, \texttt{SPOTlight}) estimate cell-type proportions across tissue spots, but unadjusted posterior credible intervals suffer severe miscalibration under atlas-to-query distribution shifts. We demonstrate \textbf{SpotGuard}, an operational, interactive conformal gatekeeping system for ST deconvolution. SpotGuard formulates nonconformity via Total Variation (TV) geometry on the probability simplex and adapts to distribution shifts via density-ratio weighted conformal prediction. Integrated into a responsive web interface with live miscoverage ($\alpha$) slider controls, SpotGuard dynamically prunes uncalibrated tissue spots, achieving 89.8\% empirical coverage on controlled shift benchmarks where Bayesian joint regions collapse.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Spatial transcriptomics, deconvolution, conformal prediction, distribution shift, oncology.
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Clinical Problem \& Importance.}
Spatial transcriptomics (ST) enables spatial gene expression profiling across intact histological tumor biopsies~\citep{marx2021method, rao2021exploring}. Because sequencing-based ST captures multi-cellular mixtures ($55\,\mu\text{m}$ spots, 5--30 cells), computational deconvolution methods—such as \texttt{cell2location}~\citep{kleshchevnikov2022cell2location}, \texttt{RCTD}~\citep{cable2021robust}, and \texttt{SPOTlight}~\citep{elosua2021spotlight}—infer cell-type proportion vectors $\hat{y} \in \simplex$. In clinical oncology, these deconvolution maps guide critical decisions, including quantifying cytotoxic CD8$^+$ T-cell infiltration, detecting tertiary lymphoid structures (TLS), and delineating surgical resection margins.

\paragraph{Failure Mode of Current Approaches.}
Deconvolution models rely on reference scRNA-seq atlases. When reference atlases and patient tissues diverge due to sequencing platform (e.g., 10x Chromium vs.\ Slide-seq), tissue preservation (FFPE vs.\ fresh frozen), or patient demographics, exchangeability fails. Standard Bayesian posteriors become severely overconfident: clinicians cannot distinguish correct from erroneous spot inferences, risking misclassifying immune-cold tumors as inflamed or declaring clear margins on invaded tissue.

\paragraph{Intended Use \& Target Population.}
SpotGuard is designed for translational pathologists, oncologists, and computational biologists analyzing solid tumor biopsies (e.g., breast carcinoma, melanoma, colorectal cancer). SpotGuard operates as an interactive conformal gatekeeper deployed alongside deconvolution pipelines to prevent uncalibrated predictions from entering clinical workflows.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Compositional Nonconformity on the Simplex.}
Cell proportions inhabit the closed simplex $\simplex = \{ v \in \mathbb{R}_+^K : \sum_{k=1}^K v_k = 1 \}$. Because sparse proportions cause log-ratio singularities at zero, SpotGuard formulates nonconformity via Total Variation (TV) distance: $\mathcal{S}_{\text{TV}}(x, y) = \frac{1}{2} \|y - \hat{y}\|_1 = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{k=1}^K |y_k - \hat{y}_k| \in [0, 1]$, defined everywhere on the closed simplex.

\paragraph{Pseudo-Spot Calibration Procedure.}
Because single-cell ground truth is unobservable in physical spatial spots, SpotGuard synthesizes $M=1{,}000$ calibration pseudo-spots $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}} = \{(x_i^{\text{syn}}, y_i^{\text{syn}})\}_{i=1}^M$ from reference single cells by drawing mixtures $y_i^{\text{syn}} \sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\beta)$, aggregating counts $N_i \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)$, injecting ambient RNA $\rho \sim \mathrm{Beta}(a, b)$, and matching empirical spatial library sizes.

\paragraph{Weighted Conformal Prediction under Covariate Shift.}
To correct for atlas-to-query distribution shifts ($P_{\text{ref}}(X) \neq P_{\text{query}}(X)$), SpotGuard trains a regularized logistic discriminator $d_\phi(x)$ on spot embeddings to compute density-ratio weights $w(x) = \frac{d_\phi(x)}{1 - d_\phi(x)} \frac{M_{\text{ref}}}{M_{\text{query}}}$~\citep{tibshirani2019conformal, barber2023conformal}. We enforce a Realism Gate ($0.60 \le \text{AUC} \le 0.80$) and clip weights at the 95th percentile to protect Effective Sample Size ($\text{ESS} = \frac{(\sum w)^2}{\sum w^2} = 314.5$). For query spot $x_{n+1}$, the weighted $(1-\alpha)$ quantile $\hat{q}_\alpha(x_{n+1})$ defines an approximately valid prediction set~\citep{angelopoulos2021gentle}:
\begin{equation}
\vspace{-2pt}
    C_\alpha(x_{n+1}) = \left\{ y \in \simplex : \mathcal{S}_{\text{TV}}(x_{n+1}, y) \le \hat{q}_\alpha(x_{n+1}) \right\}.
\vspace{-2pt}
\end{equation}
SpotGuard gates spots via decision ambiguity: masking spot $x$ iff $C_\alpha(x)$ straddles a clinical threshold (e.g., CD8$^+$ infiltration $> 5\%$).

\paragraph{System Architecture \& Deployment.}
SpotGuard couples a Python engine with a zero-dependency interactive Canvas frontend on GitHub Pages, achieving sub-16ms ($>60\,\text{fps}$) recomputation during slider interaction.

\paragraph{Baseline Analysis \& Shift Performance.}
The Bayesian Joint CI is an unadjusted product of marginal posterior intervals, collapsing geometrically on $\simplex$ for moderate $K$, while marginal CIs achieve 85.4\% coverage. Under mild cross-donor shift, weighting matches unweighted coverage (89.8\%) at a slight efficiency cost ($\mathcal{V}=0.058$ vs.\ $0.054$). Under cross-platform shift, SpotGuard prevents a 5.2\% coverage drop ($89.4\%$ vs.\ $84.6\%$), achieving an AURC of 0.138.

\paragraph{Planned Clinical Validation.}
We are validating SpotGuard on 10x Visium breast carcinoma sections to test whether conformal gatekeeping prunes artifactual CD8$^+$ margin calls against orthogonal multiplex immunofluorescence co-staining.
