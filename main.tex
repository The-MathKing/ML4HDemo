\begin{abstract}
Spatial transcriptomics (ST) deconvolution models (such as \texttt{cell2location} and \texttt{RCTD}) predict cell-type proportions across spots on a tissue. We propose \textbf{SpotGuard}, an operational, interactive conformal gatekeeping system for ST deconvolution. The SpotGuard nonconformity formulation relies on the Total Variation (TV) geometry on the simplex $\simplex$. For adaptation to an atlas shift, SpotGuard uses a density-ratio weighted conformal prediction method. We implement SpotGuard within a sub-16ms interactive workstation, equipped with live miscoverage ($\alpha$) slider controls, which automatically prunes uncalibrated spots, recovering 89.4\% coverage in response to a severe atlas shift (vs.\ 84.6\% unweighted, while Bayesian joint intervals collapse to 1.4\%) and decreasing compositional Total Variation error on retained tissue by 66.2\% ($\text{AURC} = 0.138$).
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
Spatial transcriptomics, deconvolution, conformal prediction, distribution shift, oncology.
\end{keywords}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{Clinical Problem \& Importance.}
Spatial transcriptomics profiles gene expression across intact biopsies~\citep{marx2021method, rao2021exploring}. Since sequencing measures multi-cellular mixtures ($55\,\mu\text{m}$ spots containing 5 to 30 cells), computational deconvolution models infer cell-type proportion vectors $\hat{y} \in \simplex$. In oncology, deconvolution maps help critical decisions: measuring cytotoxic CD8$^+$ T-cell infiltration, identifying tertiary lymphoid structures, and describing surgical margins.

\paragraph{Failure Mode of Current Approaches.}
Deconvolution models use reference single-cell RNA sequencing atlases. If atlases and patient tissues do not match due to platform differences (such as 10x Chromium vs.\ Slide-seq), tissue preservation (FFPE vs.\ fresh frozen), or demographics, exchangeability fails. Bayesian posteriors become overconfident: physicians have no way to tell between good and bad spot calls, potentially proclaiming clean margins on invaded tissue.

\paragraph{Intended Use \& Target Population.}
SpotGuard targets translational pathologists and computational oncologists analyzing solid tumor biopsies, operating as an interactive conformal gatekeeper deployed alongside deconvolution pipelines to prune uncalibrated spot calls.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Compositional Nonconformity on the Simplex.}
Cell proportions live on the closed unit simplex $\simplex = \{ v \in \mathbb{R}_+^K : \sum_{k=1}^K v_k = 1 \}$ with proportions summing to one. SpotGuard computes base predictions through the maximum a posteriori solution for Dirichlet-multinomial distributions~\citep{kleshchevnikov2022cell2location}. In practice, we find that boundary log singularities make sparse proportions ill-defined, so we define nonconformity as the Total Variation (TV) distance: $\mathcal{S}_{\text{TV}}(x, y) = \frac{1}{2} \|y - \hat{y}\|_1 = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{k=1}^K |y_k - \hat{y}_k| \in [0, 1]$, which is well-defined everywhere on the closed simplex.

\paragraph{Pseudo-Spot Calibration Procedure.}
Since real tissue spots do not have available physical single-cell ground truth, SpotGuard generates $M=1{,}000$ calibration pseudo-spots $\mathcal{D}_{\text{cal}} = \{(x_i^{\text{syn}}, y_i^{\text{syn}})\}_{i=1}^M$ from reference single cells by sampling over Dirichlet cell-type mixtures $y_i^{\text{syn}} \sim \mathrm{Dirichlet}(\beta)$, aggregating Poisson transcript counts $N_i \sim \mathrm{Poisson}(\lambda)$, adding ambient contamination $\rho \sim \mathrm{Beta}(a, b)$, and matching observed sequencing library depth.

\paragraph{Weighted Conformal Prediction under Covariate Shift.}
To adjust for covariate shift between atlas and query data ($P_{\text{ref}}(X) \neq P_{\text{query}}(X)$), SpotGuard learns a regularized discriminator $d_\phi(x)$ on PCA spot embeddings to predict density-ratio weights $w(x) = \frac{d_\phi(x)}{1 - d_\phi(x)} \frac{M_{\text{ref}}}{M_{\text{query}}}$~\citep{tibshirani2019conformal, barber2023conformal}. We require a 5-fold cross-validated Realism Gate ($0.60 \le \text{AUC} \le 0.80$) and clip weights at the 95th percentile to safeguard the Effective Sample Size ($\text{ESS} = 314.5$). Each query spot receives a weighted quantile defining a valid simplex prediction ball~\citep{angelopoulos2021gentle}:
\begin{equation}
\vspace{-3pt}
    C_\alpha(x_{n+1}) = \left\{ y \in \simplex : \mathcal{S}_{\text{TV}}(x_{n+1}, y) \le \hat{q}_\alpha(x_{n+1}) \right\}.
\vspace{-3pt}
\end{equation}
Under joint distribution shift, worst-case coverage error is strictly bounded by the total variation distance between reference and query distributions: $|\mathbb{P}_{(X,Y)\sim Q}(y \in C_\alpha(X)) - (1-\alpha)| \le d_{\mathrm{TV}}(P_{X,Y}, Q_{X,Y})$~\citep{barber2023conformal}.

\paragraph{Selective Gatekeeping Mechanism.}
SpotGuard's selective gatekeeping mechanism flags and masks a spot if and only if its prediction set covers a critical treatment threshold (e.g., $CD8^+ \ge 5\%$) or surpasses the safe uncertainty radius $\tau_{\text{safe}}$.

\paragraph{System Architecture \& Deployment.}
We implement SpotGuard using a Python calibration backend and interactive Canvas frontend on GitHub Pages providing sub-16ms ($>60\,\text{fps}$) recomputation during live $\alpha$ tuning.

\paragraph{Shift Performance \& Gatekeeping Accuracy.}
Under cross-platform shift, density-ratio weighting prevents coverage collapse ($89.4\%$ vs.\ $84.6\%$ unweighted, $\text{AURC} = 0.138$). In mild cross-donor shift, SpotGuard retains nominal coverage ($89.8\%$) compared to unweighted conformal prediction without adding unnecessary bulk, confirming no-harm behavior under minimal shift. Critically, we show that the selective gatekeeper allows us to mask $18.4\%$ of spots with $91.3\%$ precision and $88.5\%$ recall against spots deemed out-of-coverage, resulting in a $66.2\%$ error reduction in mean compositional Total Variation error on retained tissue (from $0.142$ to $0.048$). The Bayesian Joint Confidence Interval reduces to $1.0\%$--$2.0\%$, as an unadjusted Cartesian product of marginals makes the Bayesian Joint Confidence Interval a hypercube, failing to account for the simplex sum constraint and ignoring compositional covariances, which leads to geometric over-exclusion of the true fractions.

\section{Discussion, Limitations, \& Access}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Clinical Utility \& Access.}
A single scalar uncertainty radius can provide clinically useful gatekeeping information for pathologists on intractable high-dimensional posterior distributions. Intractable covariates motivate tools that reduce them to scalars.
\textbf{Web App}: \url{https://the-mathking.github.io/ML4HDemo/} $\bullet$ 
\textbf{Video}: \url{https://youtu.be/u3NUaWljdgo} $\bullet$ 
\textbf{Code}: \url{https://github.com/The-MathKing/ML4HDemo}

\paragraph{Limitations \& Scope.}
SpotGuard uses reference single-cell pools to define this uncertainty region, which may not contain all possible rare tumor states. While density-ratio weighting addresses covariate shift in spot gene expression ($P(X)$), extreme biological label shifts ($P(Y|X)$) require ongoing validation against physical multiplex immunofluorescence co-stains.
